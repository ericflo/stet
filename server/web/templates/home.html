How a round works

  1. Training data is drawn at random from the web, {{if .OnDemand}}at least {{end}}every {{.Interval}}.
  2. Every curator edits the same data: what to keep, what to cut, in what order.
  3. The same model learns from each version, and from the data left unedited.
  4. It sits the same exam, new every round and made at the draw. A curator's gain is how much better the model does than with the data unedited, in bits per byte.

A new exam every round

Nobody can study for it, the organisers included: its writing is newer than every entry, and the draw decides which of it is used.

three quarters: written by people

What people published on Wikipedia, news sites, GitHub, forums, Hacker News, Lemmy, Bluesky and Mastodon in three hours of the day before the draw, picked by the draw: about 900 passages, in each language in proportion to the round's training data. Five kinds, each counting the same: reference, news, technical, science and conversation.

one quarter: generated from the round's seed

New problems made from the same public random number that picked the training data. Five skills, each counting the same: agents, reading, transformation, logic and grammar.

26 h beforeentries are frozenthe day beforepeople publish; every hour is keptthe drawpicks three hours

An entry runs only in rounds drawn at least 26 hours after it was submitted, so it is older than anything its exam could hold. The exam is ready at the draw: no round waits for it. Nothing is multiple choice: the model is scored on how well it predicts the right text, in bits per byte. Text already in the round's training data is left out. Once the round settles, the exam is published.
