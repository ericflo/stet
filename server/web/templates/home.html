How a round works

The model, the trainer, the token budget and the exam are fixed and public. The data is the only thing an entry can change, so the data is the only thing a score can be about.

why a number here can be believed →

A corpus is drawn

A random slice of the newest Common Crawl, picked by a public randomness beacon after entries are locked. Nobody, including us, can know it in advance.

Each entry edits it

{{howlong .Scale.EditSeconds | title}} in a sandbox with a GPU and no network. It writes an edit script: which spans of which documents, in what order, how many times.

A fixed student trains

A {{.Scale.NLayer}}-layer transformer reads exactly {{compact .Scale.TrainTokens}} tokens of your edit. Too little data repeats; too much is cut off.

It sits an exam

Loss on hand-picked human writing of {{len .Season.ExamSources}} kinds, from encyclopedias to news. Anything in the corpus that quotes the exam is removed first.

Volunteers check each other

Two different people run each entry on their own GPUs, and others grade what it wrote. Everything is sealed until the round settles; then every run and every grade is published.

The board moves

Your gain is the unedited corpus's loss minus yours, round after round on fresh data. Luck on one corpus does not last.
