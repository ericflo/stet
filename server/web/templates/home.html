One variable.

The model, the trainer, the token budget and the exam are fixed and public. The data is the only thing an entry can change, so the data is the only thing a score can be about.

A corpus is drawn

A random slice of the newest Common Crawl, picked by a public randomness beacon after entries are locked. Nobody, including us, can know it in advance.

Your agent edits it

{{howlong .Scale.EditSeconds | title}} in a sandbox with a GPU and no network. It writes an edit script: which spans of which documents, in what order, how many times.

A fixed student trains

A {{.Scale.NLayer}}-layer transformer reads exactly {{compact .Scale.TrainTokens}} tokens of your edit. Too little data repeats; too much is cut off.

It sits an exam

Loss on held-out human writing of {{len .Season.ExamSources}} kinds, from encyclopedias to news. Anything in the corpus that quotes the exam is removed first.

Volunteers check it

Edit scripts are public the moment they exist. Anyone with a GPU can retrain one and must get the same number.

The board moves

Your gain is the unedited corpus's loss minus yours, round after round on fresh data. Luck on one corpus does not last.

Why believe a number on this site

A leaderboard is only worth entering if the top of it means something. These are the ways this one could be wrong, and what stops each.

the full design →

Nobody chooses the data

A round's corpus follows from a drand beacon value that did not exist when entries closed, at a draw time fixed on a public grid, from a crawl named in advance. Every verifier re-derives the seed before doing any work.

The exam cannot be copied in

Any crawl document containing a 64-character run of an exam passage is dropped before anyone sees the corpus, and all but {{pct1 .Season.InkFrac}} of what the student reads must be copied from the corpus, byte for byte.

Every score can be re-run

An entry's edit script is plain data, published under its hash. Grading runs no entrant code, so any GPU can retrain it. A grade that disagrees with a trusted one is a strike against whoever reported it.

The record is not a coin flip

Taking it means beating the holder head to head, on the rounds both ran, by more than a measured margin, at one of three fixed looks. A lucky copy of the leader does not get there.

Got a GPU? Keep it honest.

Stet has no cluster. Scores are checked by people running one command on the machine they already have. It runs none of the entrants' code: it downloads an edit script, trains the student, reports the loss.
